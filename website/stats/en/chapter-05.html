Lecture 5: Regression Discontinuity Designs

Introduce a cutoff using a running variable \(Z_i\in\mathbb R\): \[W_i=\mathbf1_{\{Z_i\geq c\}},\qquad c\text{ is the threshold.}\] As \(Z_i\to c\), observations near \(c\) have similar characteristics and differ in whether they receive the intervention. Let \(\mu_{(w)}(z)=E[Y_i(w)\mid Z_i=z]\). If the two conditional means are continuous, the CATE at \(z=c\) is \[\tau_c=\mu_{(1)}(c)-\mu_{(0)}(c) =\lim_{z\to c^+}E[Y_i\mid Z_i=z]-\lim_{z\to c^-}E[Y_i\mid Z_i=z].\]

Personal supplement: why does ordinary IPW not apply?

Earlier we assumed unconfoundedness \(\{Y_i(0),Y_i(1)\}\perp W_i\mid Z_i\) and overlap \(\eta\leq P(W_i=1\mid Z_i)\leq1-\eta\). In RDD, unconfoundedness holds, but overlap fails because the treatment probability given \(Z_i=z\) is either zero or one. No positive \(\eta\) makes this deterministic assignment satisfy overlap. We must compare observations near \(c\), using similarity rather than overlap. In this nonparametric smoothness class, failure of overlap prevents a uniform \(\sqrt n\) rate for \(\tau_c\). Its minimax error converges at a nonparametric rate depending on the smoothness of \(\mu_{(w)}(z)\). For example, a uniformly bounded second derivative at \(c\) allows an \(n^{-2/5}\) estimation-error rate. The rate uses independent observations, positive continuous design density on both sides of \(c\), a second-derivative bound near \(c\), and bounded conditional variance. Stronger parametric restrictions can give different rates.

Estimation via local linear regression

Choose a small bandwidth \(h_n\to0\) and a symmetric weighting function \(K\). Fit the two conditional means locally. The coefficient on \(W_i\) in \[\arg\min_{a,\tau,\beta_{(0)},\beta_{(1)}}\sum_i K\left(\frac{|Z_i-c|}{h_n}\right) [Y_i-a-\tau W_i-\beta_{(0)}(Z_i-c)_--\beta_{(1)}(Z_i-c)_+]^2\] is \(\widehat\tau_c\).

Weight; negative side; positive side \(\textcolor{red}{\longrightarrow}\) \(K(|Z_i-c|/h_n)\); \((Z_i-c)_-\); \((Z_i-c)_+\).

The intercept \(a\) and slopes \(\beta_{(w)}\) are nuisance parameters. Common kernels are the window function \(K(x)=\mathbf1_{\{|x|\leq1\}}\) and triangular kernel \(K(x)=(1-|x|)_+\).

Estimator properties and smoothness

Quantify continuity by assuming \(\mu_{(w)}\) is twice differentiable with a uniformly bounded second derivative: \[\left|\frac{d^2}{dz^2}\mu_{(w)}(z)\right|\leq B, \qquad z\in\mathbb R,\quad w\in\{0,1\}.\] With only Lipschitz smoothness, \(|f(x)-f(y)|\le L|x-y|\), functions may have corners, and local averaging can be appropriate when a local linear trend is unhelpful. If smoothness is stronger, with bounded derivatives of order \(k\geq3\), higher-order local regression may improve the convergence rate.

Taylor-expand at \(c\): \[\mu_{(w)}(z)=a_{(w)}+\beta_{(w)}(z-c)+\tfrac12\rho_{(w)}(z-c), \qquad |\rho_{(w)}(u)|\leq Bu^2.\] The last term is the remainder, and \(\tau_c=a_{(1)}-a_{(0)}\). Fit each side separately: \[(\widehat a_{(1)},\widehat\beta_{(1)})=\arg\min_{a,\beta} \sum_{Z_i\geq c}K\left(\frac{|Z_i-c|}{h_n}\right)[Y_i-a-\beta(Z_i-c)]^2,\] and analogously on the control side; then \(\widehat\tau_c=\widehat a_{(1)}-\widehat a_{(0)}\).

Closed-form weights for the window kernel.

On \(c\leq Z_i\leq c+h_n\), write \[\widehat a_{(1)}=\sum_i\gamma_iY_i,\qquad \gamma_i=\frac{m_2-m_1(Z_i-c)}{N_+(m_2-m_1^2)},\] where \(N_+=|\{i:c\le Z_i\le c+h_n\}|\) and \(m_j=N_+^{-1}\sum_{c\le Z_i\le c+h_n}(Z_i-c)^j\). Assume \(m_2-m_1^2>0\). The factor \(N_+\) normalizes the weights, so

\[\sum_i\gamma_i=1,\qquad \sum_i\gamma_i(Z_i-c)=0.\]

The decomposition is \[\widehat a_{(1)}=a_{(1)}+\underbrace{\tfrac12\sum_i\gamma_i\rho_{(1)}(Z_i-c)}_{\text{\textcolor{red}{curvature bias}}} +\underbrace{\sum_i\gamma_i(Y_i-\mu_{(1)}(Z_i))}_{\text{\textcolor{red}{sampling noise}}}.\] The control intercept has an analogous decomposition. Local-linear weights can be negative, so the absolute curvature bias is bounded by \(\frac B2\sum_i|\gamma_i|(Z_i-c)^2\). With regular local design this is \(O(h_n^2)\).

Curvature bias and sampling noise \(\textcolor{red}{\longrightarrow}\) Both terms need bounds.

Curvature bias is bounded at order \(Bh_n^2\), where \(B\) bounds the second derivative and \(h_n\) is the bandwidth. Sampling noise has mean zero; if \(\operatorname{Var}[Y_i\mid Z_i]\leq b^2\), its variance is bounded by \(b^2\sum_i\gamma_i^2\). If \(Z_i\) has a continuous nonzero density \(f\) near \(c\), \[b^2\sum_i\gamma_i^2\approx\frac{4b^2}{|\{i:c\leq Z_i\leq c+h_n\}|} \approx\frac{4b^2}{f(c)nh_n}.\] Thus squared bias is of order \(h_n^4\) and variance is of order \((nh_n)^{-1}\), giving \[\widehat\tau_c=\tau_c+O_p(n^{-2/5}),\qquad h_n\asymp n^{-1/5}.\] Here \(n^{-2/5}\) is the estimation error. With \(k\)-th order smoothness, \(h_n\asymp n^{-1/(2k+1)}\).

Noise in the running variable

May contain noise \(\textcolor{blue}{\longrightarrow}\) The running variable \(Z_i\).

Introduce a latent \(U_i\sim G\) and two conditions:

  1. A noisy running variable: assume \(Z_i\mid U_i\sim N(U_i,\nu^2)\), with \(\nu>0\).

  2. Unconfoundedness of the running variable: \(\{Y_i(0),Y_i(1)\}\perp Z_i\mid U_i\).

With \(Y_i=Y_i(W_i)\) and \(\{Y_i(0),Y_i(1)\}\perp W_i\mid U_i\), \[e(u)=P(W_i=1\mid U_i=u)=P(Z_i\geq c\mid U_i=u) =1-\Phi\left(\frac{c-u}{\nu}\right),\] \[\alpha_{(w)}(u)=E[Y_i(w)\mid U_i=u],\qquad \tau(u)=E[Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)\mid U_i=u].\] The annotation defines the standard normal CDF, The propensity expression follows from \(Z=U+\nu\xi\), with \(\xi\sim N(0,1)\) independent of \(U\). Weight normalization alone does not remove confounding: the second integral vanishes when \(h_+=h_-\), or under another condition making it zero.

\(\Phi(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_{-\infty}^x e^{-t^2/2}\,dt\).

\(U_i\) is unobserved, so we cannot directly use its propensity score. Consider a deconvolution-type estimator:

Unobserved \(U_i\): cannot directly use its propensity score \(\textcolor{black}{\longrightarrow}\) Deconvolution weights \(\gamma_+(z),\gamma_-(z)\).

\[\widehat\tau_\gamma=\frac1n\sum_{Z_i\geq c}\gamma_+(Z_i)Y_i -\frac1n\sum_{Z_i<c}\gamma_-(Z_i)Y_i.\] Choose the weights to satisfy \[E[\mathbf1_{\{Z_i\geq c\}}\gamma_+(Z_i)] =E[\mathbf1_{\{Z_i<c\}}\gamma_-(Z_i)]=1.\] Then \[E[\widehat\tau_\gamma] =\underbrace{\int h_+(u)\tau(u)\,dG(u)}_{\text{\textcolor{red}{weighted treatment effect}}} +\underbrace{\int(h_+(u)-h_-(u))\alpha_{(0)}(u)\,dG(u)}_{\text{\textcolor{red}{confounding bias}}},\] where the weighting functions are \[h_+(u)=\int_c^\infty\gamma_+(z)\varphi_\nu(z-u)\,dz,\qquad h_-(u)=\int_{-\infty}^c\gamma_-(z)\varphi_\nu(z-u)\,dz,\]

\[\varphi_\nu(z-u)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi\nu^2}}\exp\left[-\frac12\left(\frac{z-u}{\nu}\right)^2\right].\]
