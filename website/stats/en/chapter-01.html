Lecture 1: Randomized Controlled Trials

Regression in an RCT analysis of the average treatment effect reduces variance; it does not play a role in identifying the ATE.

Regression in an RCT \(\textcolor{black}{\longrightarrow}\) Variance reduction; ATE identification comes from assignment.

\[\Delta_i=Y_i(1)-Y_i(0),\qquad \tau=E[Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)].\] Estimate and construct inference for \(\tau\) using the difference-in-means estimator: \[\widehat\tau_{\rm DM}=\frac1{n_1}\sum_{W_i=1}Y_i-\frac1{n_0}\sum_{W_i=0}Y_i, \qquad n_w=|\{i:W_i=w\}|.\] This estimator is consistent and unbiased under random assignment.

Difference in means \(\textcolor{blue}{\longrightarrow}\) \(\widehat\tau_{\rm DM}\)

Assume IID sampling, finite second moments, and nonempty treatment groups. This lecture uses balanced assignment \(P(W=1)=1/2\) and centered covariates \(E[X]=0\). Normal approximations are asymptotic.

Supplement: assumptions.

\[Y_i=Y_i(W_i),\qquad W_i\perp\{Y_i(0),Y_i(1)\}.\] The second condition is random assignment. SUTVA requires no interference between units and a well-defined version of each specified treatment. Together with consistency, this gives \(Y_i=Y_i(W_i)\). Independent sampling is a separate assumption.

\(Y_i=Y_i(W_i)\); SUTVA \(\textcolor{black}{\longrightarrow}\) No interference and well-defined treatments.

Linear outcome model and variance reduction

\[Y_i(w)=c_{(w)}+X_i\beta_{(w)}+\varepsilon_i(w),\quad E[\varepsilon_i(w)\mid X_i]=0,\quad \operatorname{Var}[\varepsilon_i(w)\mid X_i]=b^2.\] The coefficients are fitted separately for the two treatment states. Write \(A=\operatorname{Var}[X]\) and \(\|v\|_A^2=v'Av\). The asymptotic variance is \[V_{\rm DM}=4b^2+\|\beta_{(0)}+\beta_{(1)}\|_A^2 +\|\beta_{(0)}-\beta_{(1)}\|_A^2.\] Also, \[\tau=c_{(1)}-c_{(0)}+E[X](\beta_{(1)}-\beta_{(0)}),\qquad \widehat\tau_{\rm OLS}=\widehat c_{(1)}-\widehat c_{(0)} +\overline X(\widehat\beta_{(1)}-\widehat\beta_{(0)}).\] The error decomposition is \[\begin{aligned} \widehat\tau_{\rm OLS}-\tau={}&(\widehat c_{(1)}-c_{(1)})-(\widehat c_{(0)}-c_{(0)}) +\overline X(\beta_{(1)}-\beta_{(0)})\\ &+\overline X(\widehat\beta_{(1)}-\widehat\beta_{(0)}-\beta_{(1)}+\beta_{(0)}). \end{aligned}\] The first two terms have asymptotic approximations \(N(0,b^2/n_1)\) and \(N(0,b^2/n_0)\); the third has approximation \(N(0,\|\beta_{(1)}-\beta_{(0)}\|_A^2/n)\); the last is \(O_p(1/n)\). Slope-estimation uncertainty is negligible to first order. Thus \[\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{\rm OLS}-\tau)\Rightarrow N(0,V_{\rm OLS}),\qquad V_{\rm OLS}=4b^2+\|\beta_{(0)}-\beta_{(1)}\|_A^2,\] \[V_{\rm DM}=V_{\rm OLS}+\|\beta_{(0)}+\beta_{(1)}\|_A^2.\]

OLS under a general outcome regression

OLS can reduce asymptotic variance even when the outcome regression is nonlinear. Suppose \[Y_i(w)=\mu_{(w)}(X_i)+\varepsilon_i(w),\quad E[\varepsilon_i(w)\mid X_i]=0,\quad \operatorname{Var}[\varepsilon_i(w)\mid X_i]=b^2.\] For difference in means, \[\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{\rm DM}-\tau)\Rightarrow N(0,V_{\rm DM}),\quad V_{\rm DM}=4b^2+2\operatorname{Var}[\mu_{(0)}(X_i)] +2\operatorname{Var}[\mu_{(1)}(X_i)].\] For OLS, the Huber–White calculation starts from the population projection \[(c^*_{(w)},\beta^*_{(w)})=\arg\min_{c,\beta} E[(Y_i(w)-X_i\beta-c)^2].\] The asymptotic projection calculation gives \[\sqrt{n_w}\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\widehat c_{(w)}\\\widehat\beta_{(w)}\end{pmatrix} -\begin{pmatrix}c^*_{(w)}\\\beta^*_{(w)}\end{pmatrix}\right\} \Rightarrow N\left(0,Q^{-1}E[r_w^2qq^\mathsf T]Q^{-1}\right),\] Here \(c^*_{(w)}=E[Y_i(w)]\). Write \(q=(1,X^\mathsf T)^\mathsf T\), \(Q=E[qq^\mathsf T]\) is nonsingular, and \(r_w=Y(w)-c_w^*-X\beta_w^*\). The sandwich covariance need not be block diagonal: \(E[r_wX]=0\) does not imply \(E[r_w^2X]=0\). With \(E[X]=0\), the intercept variance is \(E[r_w^2]\).1

\[\operatorname{MSE}^*_{(w)}=E[(Y_i(w)-X_i\beta^*_{(w)}-c^*_{(w)})^2].\] Therefore \[\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{\rm OLS}-\tau)\Rightarrow N(0,V_{\rm OLS}),\qquad V_{\rm OLS}=V_{\rm DM}-\|\beta^*_{(0)}+\beta^*_{(1)}\|_A^2.\] \(V_{\rm OLS}\le V_{\rm DM}\) in this balanced-trial setting. Strict reduction occurs when \(\|\beta^*_{(0)}+\beta^*_{(1)}\|_A^2>0\). Equality holds when this squared norm is zero; for nonsingular \(A\), this means \(\beta^*_{(0)}+\beta^*_{(1)}=0\).

Personal observation.

OLS can be viewed as \[\widehat\tau_{\rm OLS}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \{(\widehat c_{(1)}+X_i\widehat\beta_{(1)}) -(\widehat c_{(0)}+X_i\widehat\beta_{(0)})\} =\frac1n\sum_i[\widehat\mu_{(1)}(X_i)-\widehat\mu_{(0)}(X_i)].\] Here \(\widehat\mu_{(w)}(x)\) is the fitted OLS value at \(x\). It could be replaced with another method.
