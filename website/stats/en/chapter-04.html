Lecture 4: Estimating Treatment Heterogeneity

For observations \((X_i,Y_i,W_i)\) with \(X_i\in\mathcal X\), we previously pursued the ATE \(E[Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)]\). Heterogeneity analysis instead considers the conditional average treatment effect (CATE): \[\tau(x)=E[Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)\mid X_i=x].\]

Average for a category of \(X_i\) \(\textcolor{blue}{\longrightarrow}\) \(\tau(x)=E[Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)\mid X_i=x]\).

Regularization bias

Under \(\{Y_i(0),Y_i(1)\}\perp W_i\mid X_i\), \[\tau(x)=\mu_{(1)}(x)-\mu_{(0)}(x),\qquad \mu_{(w)}(x)=E[Y_i\mid X_i=x,W_i=w].\] A direct CATE estimator is \(\widehat\tau_T(x)=\widehat\mu_{(1)}(x)-\widehat\mu_{(0)}(x)\).

Personal concern.

If there are too few observations for \(\widehat\mu_{(1)}\), it may be heavily regularized (toward a straight line). If there are many observations for \(\widehat\mu_{(0)}\), that fit may be accurate; then the difference \(\widehat\tau_T(x)\) goes wrong because of the first fit.

Semiparametric modeling

\[Y_i(w)=f(X_i)+w\tau(X_i)+\varepsilon_i(w),\qquad P(W_i=1\mid X_i)=e(X_i),\qquad \tau(x)=\psi(x)\beta.\] \(f\) is a function of \(X_i\), and \(\psi(x)\) is a nonparametric representation of \(X\); \(\beta\) is the treatment-effect coefficient. Under unconfoundedness, the Robinson transformation is

\(Y_i(w)=f(X_i)+w\tau(X_i)+\varepsilon_i(w)\) \(\textcolor{black}{\longrightarrow}\) Robinson: subtract \(m(X_i)\) and residualize \(W_i\) by \(e(X_i)\).

\[Y_i-m(X_i)=(W_i-e(X_i))\psi(X_i)\beta+\varepsilon_i, \quad m(x)=E[Y_i\mid X_i=x]=f(x)+e(x)\tau(x),\] with \(\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon_i(W_i)\).

\(\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon_i(W_i)\) \(\textcolor{blue}{\longrightarrow}\) The error in \(Y_i-m(X_i)=(W_i-e(X_i))\psi(X_i)\beta+\varepsilon_i\).

Design a residual-on-residual OLS regression using \[\widetilde Y_i^*=Y_i-m(X_i),\qquad \widetilde Z_i^*=(W_i-e(X_i))\psi(X_i).\] Regress \(\widetilde Y_i^*\) on \(\widetilde Z_i^*\) to obtain \(\widehat\beta_R^*\) . These transformations use only the observed data and the nuisance functions; this is data preprocessing. We obtain \[\sqrt n(\widehat\beta_R^*-\beta)\Rightarrow N(0,V_R),\] \[V_R=Q^{-1}E[ZZ^\mathsf T\varepsilon^2]Q^{-1}.\] Under homoskedasticity, \(\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i\mid X_i,W_i)=b^2\) is constant and \(V_R\) is the semiparametrically efficient variance for \(\beta\). Under heteroskedasticity, the sandwich variance still applies but need not attain the efficiency bound. Here \(Z=(\widetilde Z_i^*)^\mathsf T\), \(Q=E[ZZ^\mathsf T]\) is nonsingular, and \(\varepsilon=\widetilde Y_i^*-Z^\mathsf T\beta\). The normal equations give influence function \(Q^{-1}Z\varepsilon\), so the middle factor uses the regression residual. Under conditional homoskedasticity this is \(b^2Q^{-1}\).1

Feasible cross-fitted version.

The preceding regression is idealized because \(m(X_i)\) and \(e(X_i)\) are unknown. Fit nonparametric regressions \(Y\sim X\) and \(W\sim X\); omit the fold containing observation \(i\) when constructing \[\widetilde Y_i=Y_i-\widehat m^{(-k(i))}(X_i),\qquad \widetilde Z_i=(W_i-\widehat e^{(-k(i))}(X_i))\psi(X_i).\] Regress \(\widetilde Y_i\) on \(\widetilde Z_i\) to obtain \(\widehat\beta_R\). If both nuisance RMSEs are \(o_p(n^{-1/4})\), we obtain \[\sqrt n(\widehat\beta_R^*-\widehat\beta_R)\longrightarrow_p0.\]

A loss function for treatment heterogeneity

The previous estimator assumes \(\tau(x)=\psi(x)\beta\). Often the relationship is not linear, so use machine learning. With \[\mu_{(w)}(x)=E[Y(w)\mid X=x],\quad \varepsilon_i(w)=Y_i(w)-[\mu_{(0)}(X_i)+w\tau(X_i)],\] unconfoundedness yields \(E[\varepsilon_i(w)\mid X_i,W_i]=0\). Here \(\mu_{(0)}\) plays the role of the previous \(f\). Again, \[Y_i-m(X_i)=(W_i-e(X_i))\tau(X_i)+\varepsilon_i, \quad m(x)=\mu_{(0)}(x)+e(x)\tau(x),\quad \varepsilon_i=\varepsilon_i(W_i).\] Equivalently, \[\tau(\cdot)=\arg\min_{t}E[((Y_i-m(X_i))-(W_i-e(X_i))t(X_i))^2].\] If \(m\) and \(e\) are known, minimizing this gives \(\tau(\cdot)\). Indeed, \(L(t)-L(\tau)=E[e(X)(1-e(X))(t(X)-\tau(X))^2]\), so the minimizer identifies \(\tau\) where \(0<e(X)<1\), up to null sets. The oracle penalized estimator is \[\widehat\tau_R^*(\cdot)=\arg\min_t\left\{ \frac1n\sum_i[(Y_i-m(X_i))-(W_i-e(X_i))t(X_i)]^2+\Lambda_n(t)\right\}.\]

Regularize the complexity of \(\tau(\cdot)\) \(\textcolor{blue}{\longrightarrow}\) \(\Lambda_n(\tau)\): examples include lasso/ridge and neural networks.

Usually \(m\) and \(e\) are unknown, so cross-fit them; in an RCT \(e\) is known by design while \(m\) generally still needs estimation:

\[\begin{aligned} \widehat L_n(t)&=\frac1n\sum_i [(Y_i-\widehat m^{(-k(i))}(X_i))-(W_i-\widehat e^{(-k(i))}(X_i))t(X_i)]^2,\\ \widehat\tau_R&=\arg\min_t\{\widehat L_n(t)+\Lambda_n(t)\}. \end{aligned}\] For lasso, \(\widehat\tau(x)=x\widehat\beta\), with \[\widehat\beta=\arg\min_\beta\left\{\frac1n\sum_i [(Y_i-\widehat m^{(-k(i))}(X_i))-(W_i-\widehat e^{(-k(i))}(X_i))X_i\beta]^2 +\lambda\|\beta\|_1\right\}.\]

Personal supplementary observation.

With a constant treatment effect, \(Y_i(w)=f(X_i)+\tau w+\varepsilon_i\) and \(E[\varepsilon_i\mid X_i,W_i]=0\), the residual estimator is \[\widehat\tau_R=\frac{\sum_i(Y_i-\widehat m^{(-k(i))}(X_i))(W_i-\widehat e^{(-k(i))}(X_i))} {\sum_i(W_i-\widehat e^{(-k(i))}(X_i))^2}. \tag{4.5}\] The premise is that the nuisance components converge fast enough. Even if \(\tau\) is not constant, this estimator converges to a nonnegatively weighted average of \(\tau(x)\).

\(\overline Y_{(1)}-\overline Y_{(0)}\) \(\textcolor{blue}{\longrightarrow}\) The nonnegative weighted average of \(\tau(x)\) in the preceding observation.

When heterogeneity is small and overlap presents difficulties, use (4.5) as a replacement.

Constant-coefficient residual regression and its weighted-effect interpretation \(\textcolor{blue}{\longrightarrow}\) R-loss for a flexible treatment-effect function.

The population target is\[\frac{E[e(X)(1-e(X))\tau(X)]}{E[e(X)(1-e(X))]},\] with a positive denominator. It emphasizes regions of overlap and equals the ATE under constant effects or when the overlap weight and \(\tau(X)\) are uncorrelated. Deterministic-treatment regions remain unidentified.
