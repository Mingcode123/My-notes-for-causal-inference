Lecture 6: Finite-Sample Inference in RDDs

Finite-sample RDDs allow a discontinuous/discrete running variable or a more complex cutoff rule. The local-linear regression solution (6.2) can be written as a linear estimator, \[\widehat\tau_c(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^n\gamma_iY_i.\tag{6.3}\] The weights depend only on \(Z_i\).

The local-linear solution (6.2) \(\textcolor{red}{\longrightarrow}\) The linear estimator (6.3), whose properties follow below.

If \[Y_i(w)=\mu_{(w)}(Z_i)+\varepsilon_i(w),\qquad \varepsilon_i(w)\mid Z_i\sim N(0,b^2),\] then, conditional on \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\), \[\widehat\tau_c(\gamma)\sim N(\widehat\tau_c^*(\gamma),b^2\|\gamma\|_2^2), \qquad \widehat\tau_c^*(\gamma)=\sum_i\gamma_i\mu_{(W_i)}(Z_i).\] Here \(W_i=\mathbf1_{\{Z_i\geq c\}}\) and \(\|\gamma\|_2=(\gamma_1^2+\cdots+\gamma_n^2)^{1/2}\) is the Euclidean norm.

Minimax linear estimation

Minimax means minimizing the worst-case MSE. For a linear estimator, the conditional variance is directly accessible as \(b^2\|\gamma\|_2^2\) under homoskedasticity. Its bias depends on unknown conditional means: \[\operatorname{Bias}(\widehat\tau_c(\gamma)\mid Z_1,\ldots,Z_n) =\sum_i\gamma_i\mu_{(W_i)}(Z_i)-[\mu_{(1)}(c)-\mu_{(0)}(c)].\] A smoothness restriction \(|\mu_{(w)}''(z)|\leq B\) bounds the bias by \[I_B(\gamma)=\sup_{|\mu_{(w)}''|\leq B} \left\{\sum_i\gamma_i\mu_{(W_i)}(Z_i)-[\mu_{(1)}(c)-\mu_{(0)}(c)]\right\}.\] The supremum does not need an absolute value because the second-derivative class is symmetric. Hence \[|\operatorname{Bias}(\widehat\tau_c(\gamma)\mid Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)|\leq I_B(\gamma),\] \[\operatorname{MSE}(\widehat\tau_c(\gamma)\mid Z_1,\ldots,Z_n) \leq b^2\|\gamma\|_2^2+I_B^2(\gamma).\] MSE = variance + squared bias; this bound does not depend on the unknown \(\mu\). Define \[\gamma^B=\arg\min_\gamma\{b^2\|\gamma\|_2^2+I_B^2(\gamma)\},\qquad \widehat\tau_c(\gamma^B)=\sum_i\gamma_i^B Y_i. \tag{6.8}\] Under the same curvature restriction, the optimized worst-case MSE is no larger than that of local-linear estimation. Condition on the design and assume independent Gaussian errors with known variance \(b^2\) and a valid curvature bound \(B\). The minimax criterion minimizes worst-case MSE over this class, not MSE at every regression function. Discrete designs require feasible constraints; intervals may retain positive width.

Deriving the minimax linear weights

Decompose \[\mu_{(w)}(z)=a_{(w)}+\beta_{(w)}(z-c)+\rho_{(w)}(z),\] where \(\rho_{(w)}(c)=\rho'_{(w)}(c)=0\) and the second derivative is bounded by \(B\). The intercepts and slopes are unrestricted, so \(I_B(\gamma)\) can be infinite unless \[\sum_i\gamma_iW_i=1,\qquad \sum_i\gamma_i=0,\qquad \sum_i\gamma_i(Z_i-c)_+=0,\qquad \sum_i\gamma_i(Z_i-c)_-=0.\] After these constraints, only the remainders need control. Rewrite (6.8) as \[(\gamma^B,t)=\arg\min_{\gamma,t}\{b^2\|\gamma\|_2^2+B^2t^2\},\] subject to \[\sum_i\gamma_iW_i\rho_{(1)}(Z_i)+\sum_i\gamma_i(1-W_i)\rho_{(0)}(Z_i)\leq t\] for every \(\rho_{(w)}\) with \(\rho_{(w)}(c)=\rho'_{(w)}(c)=0\) and \(|\rho''_{(w)}(z)|\leq1\), together with \[\sum_i\gamma_iW_i=1,\quad\sum_i\gamma_i=0,\quad \sum_i\gamma_iW_i(Z_i-c)=0,\quad \sum_i\gamma_i(1-W_i)(Z_i-c)=0. \tag{6.10}\] Solve the corresponding dual problem.

Primal minimax optimization (6.10) \(\textcolor{blue}{\longrightarrow}\) Transform to the dual problem to solve it.

Personal supplement: why do the weights sum to zero?

The final estimate is a difference. Split its sum into the treated and control parts: \[\widehat\tau_c(\gamma)=\sum_i\gamma_i^{(1)}Y_i+\sum_i\gamma_i^{(2)}Y_i.\] The treated weights sum to \(1\) and the control weights sum to \(-1\), so

\(W=1\) group; \(W=0\) group \(\textcolor{red}{\longrightarrow}\) Weights sum to \(1\) and \(-1\), respectively; total sum \(0\).

\[\sum_i\gamma_i=1+(-1)=0,\quad \sum_i\gamma_i^{(1)}=\sum_i\gamma_iW_i=1,\quad \sum_i\gamma_i^{(2)}=\sum_i\gamma_i(1-W_i)=-1.\]

The sketch shows a weight profile with a negative spike just to the left of the threshold and a positive spike just to the right; farther away, the weights change sign and then approach zero.

Inference with linear estimators

Construct a confidence interval for \(\widehat\tau_c(\gamma^B)\). Let \[\mathrm{err}=\widehat\tau_c-\tau_c,\qquad \mathrm{err}\mid Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\sim N(\mathrm{bias},b^2\|\gamma^B\|_2^2),\] where \(|\mathrm{bias}|\leq Bt\) from (6.10). By unimodality of the Gaussian distribution, for \(S\sim N(0,1)\), \[P(|\mathrm{err}|\geq\zeta)\leq P(|Bt+b\|\gamma^B\|_2S|\geq\zeta).\] A confidence interval with conditional coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) is \[I_\alpha=(\widehat\tau_c(\gamma^B)-\zeta_\alpha^B, \widehat\tau_c(\gamma^B)+\zeta_\alpha^B),\] \[\zeta_\alpha^B=\inf\{\zeta:P(|Bt+b\|\gamma^B\|_2S|>\zeta)\leq\alpha\}. \tag{6.13}\] Conditioning on \(Z_i\) makes this work for running variables with any distribution, including nonstandard RDD settings.

Discrete running variables.

The restriction \(|\mu_{(w)}''(z)|\leq B\) alone need not identify \(\tau_c\), but (6.13) remains valid. For continuous \(Z_i\), the interval shrinks at rate \(n^{-2/5}\); for discrete \(Z_i\), its length may not tend to zero. When \(c\) is outside the support of the running variable, one needs an interpolating function representing \(\mu_{(w)}(z)\) and satisfying the second-derivative bound.

Multivariate running variables.

Examples include two- or three-dimensional regions and geographic boundaries. A scalar-cutoff RDD cannot be used directly; some reduce the problem to one-dimensional distance, which can lose relevant spatial information. The minimax method can instead be extended directly to multiple dimensions.

Beyond homoskedasticity

When the errors \(\varepsilon_i=Y_i-\mu_{(W_i)}(Z_i)\) are no longer homoskedastic Gaussian with variance \(b^2\), the exact normal statement (6.11) fails. Use a central limit theorem to justify \[\widehat\tau_c(\gamma)\mid Z_1,\ldots,Z_n \ \approx\ N\left(\widehat\tau_c^*(\gamma), \sum_i\gamma_i^2\operatorname{Var}[Y_i\mid Z_i,W_i]\right). \tag{6.14}\] With a valid Gaussian approximation, confidence intervals can still be obtained. Estimate the conditional variance by \[\widehat V_n=\sum_i\gamma_i^2(Y_i-\widehat\mu_{(W_i)}(Z_i))^2,\] where the fitted mean comes from local-linear regression.

Local-linear fitted mean \(\textcolor{blue}{\longrightarrow}\) \(\widehat\mu_{(W_i)}(Z_i)\) in the residual-variance estimator.

On independent evaluation data, if the fitted mean is wrong \(\textcolor{blue}{\longrightarrow}\) Expected squared residuals increase by the squared mean error.

The minimax criterion (6.8) was for homoskedasticity; under heteroskedasticity, introduce individual variances \(b_i^2\) into (6.10), the problem used to construct the dual. The residual-inflation intuition applies to independent evaluation data: expected squared residual is conditional variance plus squared mean error. In-sample overfitting can shrink residuals. Use valid leave-one-out or cross-fitted variance estimation and a weighted CLT with no dominant observation.
