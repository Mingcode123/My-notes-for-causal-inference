Lecture 11: Policy learning

We study how to allocate treatment so as to maximize benefit. The decision is simply which group a person should enter: treatment or control.

A treatment policy is a mapping \[\pi:\mathcal X\longrightarrow\{0,1\}.\] An individual with characteristics \(X_i=x\) is treated if and only if \(\pi(x)=1\). The goal is to find a policy that maximizes expected utility: \[V(\pi)=\mathbb E[Y_i(\pi(X_i))].\] (Take \(Y_i=1\) to be a good outcome.) For a policy class \(\Pi\), define \[\pi^*=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi'\in\Pi}V(\pi').\] The regret of another policy is \[R(\pi)=\sup_{\pi'\in\Pi}V(\pi')-V(\pi).\] the loss is regret, rather than squared-error loss. The objective is to learn a policy with a guaranteed upper bound on worst-case regret: a minimax regret criterion.

Assume IID observations \((X_i,Y_i,W_i)\in\mathcal X\times\mathbb R\times\{0,1\}\), \(i=1,\ldots,n\), with \[\{Y_i(0),Y_i(1)\}\perp W_i\mid X_i,\qquad Y_i=Y_i(W_i),\] \[0<\eta\le e(X_i)\le1-\eta<1,\qquad e(x)=\mathbb P(W_i=1\mid X_i=x).\] After learning \(\widehat\pi\), set \(W_i=\widehat\pi(X_i)\) and expect \(Y_i=Y_i(\widehat\pi(X_i))\) to be large. My observation: once treatment is assigned this way, assignment has no randomness; we can no longer learn the causal effect from it in the same way.

Deployment data alone lose overlap: \(e(x)\in\{0,1\}\). Earlier randomized data or additional assumptions may still support causal learning.

Policy learning by empirical maximization

In general, \[\widehat\pi=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi\in\Pi}\widehat V(\pi).\] If \(e(x)\) is known, an unbiased IPW choice is \[\widehat V_{\mathrm{IPW}}(\pi) =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\mathbf1\{W_i=\pi(X_i)\}Y_i} {\mathbb P[W_i=\pi(X_i)\mid X_i]}, \qquad \widehat\pi_{\mathrm{IPW}} =\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi\in\Pi}\widehat V_{\mathrm{IPW}}(\pi).\] In words, retain observations whose treatment \(W_i\) agrees with the policy \(\pi(X_i)\), and weight their observed outcomes using the inverse assignment probability. Unconfoundedness gives \[\begin{aligned} \mathbb E[\widehat V_{\mathrm{IPW}}(\pi)] &=\mathbb E\left[ \frac{\mathbf1\{W_i=\pi(X_i)\}Y_i(\pi(X_i))} {\mathbb P[W_i=\pi(X_i)\mid X_i]}\right]\\ &=\mathbb E\left[ \mathbb E\left[\left. \frac{\mathbf1\{W_i=\pi(X_i)\}} {\mathbb P[W_i=\pi(X_i)\mid X_i]}\right|X_i\right] \mathbb E[Y_i(\pi(X_i))\mid X_i]\right]\\ &=\mathbb E[Y_i(\pi(X_i))]=V(\pi). \end{aligned}\] The conditional expectation of the indicator divided by its probability is \(1\). \[\textcolor{red}{\boxed{\textcolor{black}{E\left[\left. \frac{\mathbf1\{W_i=\pi(X_i)\}}{P[W_i=\pi(X_i)\mid X_i]} \right|X_i\right]}}}=\textcolor{red}{1}.\]

Analogy: unbiasedness of the ATE IPW estimator.

\[\begin{aligned} \mathbb E[\widehat\tau^*_{\mathrm{IPW}}] &=\mathbb E\left[\frac{W_iY_i}{e(X_i)}- \frac{(1-W_i)Y_i}{1-e(X_i)}\right]\\ &=\mathbb E\left[\frac{W_iY_i(1)}{e(X_i)}- \frac{(1-W_i)Y_i(0)}{1-e(X_i)}\right]\\ &=\mathbb E\left[ \mathbb E\left[\left.\frac{W_iY_i(1)}{e(X_i)}\right|e(X_i)\right] -\mathbb E\left[\left.\frac{(1-W_i)Y_i(0)}{1-e(X_i)}\right|e(X_i)\right] \right]\\ &=\mathbb E[Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)]. \end{aligned}\] Thus it is unbiased for \(\tau\).

Policy learning as weighted classification

Decompose the value function: \[V(\pi)=\underbrace{\mathbb E[Y_i(0)]}_{\textcolor{red}{E_0}}+ \mathbb E[(Y_i(1)-Y_i(0))\pi(X_i)].\] My interpretation: this displays the baseline outcome \(E_0\) and the contribution from the treatment effects of people treated by \(\pi(\cdot)\).

With \(\tau(x)\) denoting the CATE, the policy advantage is \[\begin{aligned} A(\pi)&=2\mathbb E[Y_i(\pi(X_i))]-\mathbb E[Y_i(0)+Y_i(1)]\\ &=\mathbb E[(2\pi(X_i)-1)\tau(X_i)]. \end{aligned}\] \(A(\pi)\) is the advantage of \(\pi(\cdot)\). The same \(\pi^*\) maximizes \(A(\pi)\) over \(\Pi\).

Rewrite IPW: \(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{IPW}}\) maximizes \[\begin{aligned} \widehat A_{\mathrm{IPW}}(\pi) &=2\widehat V_{\mathrm{IPW}}(\pi) -\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\left\{ \frac{W_iY_i}{e(X_i)}+\frac{(1-W_i)Y_i}{1-e(X_i)}\right\}\\ &=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(2\pi(X_i)-1) \left\{\frac{W_iY_i}{e(X_i)}- \frac{(1-W_i)Y_i}{1-e(X_i)}\right\}. \end{aligned} \tag{11.10}\] For fixed \(\pi\), this estimates the advantage without bias.

This unbiasedness statement is for a fixed policy, or a policy learned from independent data; maximizing the estimate on the same sample need not preserve unbiasedness.

Write \[\widehat A_{\mathrm{IPW}}(\pi) =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(2\pi(X_i)-1)\Gamma_i^{\mathrm{IPW}}, \qquad \Gamma_i^{\mathrm{IPW}}= \frac{W_iY_i}{e(X_i)}-\frac{(1-W_i)Y_i}{1-e(X_i)}.\tag{11.11}\] Here \(\Gamma_i^{\mathrm{IPW}}\) is the IPW score used in ATE estimation: \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{IPW}}=n^{-1}\sum_i\Gamma_i^{\mathrm{IPW}}\). My interpretation: \(\widehat A_{\mathrm{IPW}}(\pi)\) resembles the ATE IPW estimator. When \(\pi(X_i)=1\), we “earn” that individual’s treatment effect; when \(\pi(X_i)=0\), we “pay” that individual’s treatment effect.

For optimization, write \[\widehat A_{\mathrm{IPW}}(\pi) =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \underbrace{(2\pi(X_i)-1)\operatorname{sign}(\Gamma_i)}_{\textcolor{red}{\text{classification target}}} \underbrace{|\Gamma_i|}_{\textcolor{red}{\text{weight }\star}}.\tag{11.12}\] The sign defines a classification target and \(|\Gamma_i|\) is the weight . Maximizing the advantage is equivalent to optimizing a weighted classification objective. In practice one often minimizes a weighted surrogate loss instead. But the estimator may be wrong. See Wager (2020), On regression tables for policy learning: Comment on a paper by Jiang, Song, Li and Zeng.

The exact score minimizes weighted classification error because \((2\pi(X_i)-1)\Gamma_i=|\Gamma_i|[1-2\mathbf1\{\pi(X_i)\ne\mathbf1\{\Gamma_i>0\}\}]\). A surrogate loss need not retain that optimizer within a restricted policy class.

Supplement: complexity and a regret rate.

The VC dimension measures the complexity of \(\Pi\); look up its precise definition. If \(|Y_i|\le M\), \(\eta\le e(X_i)\le1-\eta\) (so the scores are bounded), and \(\Pi\) has finite Vapnik–Chervonenkis dimension, then \[R(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{IPW}}) =O_P\left(\frac M\eta\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{VC}(\Pi)}n}\right), \quad \widehat\pi_{\mathrm{IPW}}= \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi\in\Pi}\widehat A_{\mathrm{IPW}}(\pi).\] This is an optimal convergence rate. What about superefficiency: after fixing \(\pi(x)\), could convergence with \(n\) observations be faster than \(\sqrt{\operatorname{VC}(\Pi)/n}\)?

Here the rate is a worst-case learning rate. Faster regret at an easier distribution is compatible with it; estimating the value of a fixed policy is a separate problem.

Efficient scoring rules for policy learning

Is there a better learning method? Observe that \[\begin{aligned} A(\pi)&=2\mathbb E[Y_i(\pi(X_i))]-\mathbb E[Y_i(0)+Y_i(1)]\\ &=\mathbb E[Y_i(\pi(X_i))]-\mathbb E[Y_i(1-\pi(X_i))]. \end{aligned}\] Thus \(A(\pi)\) is the ATE in an experiment comparing deployment of \(\pi(\cdot)\) with deployment of its opposite policy.

Oracle IPW is feasible but inefficient. The oracle AIPW estimator uses an efficient score and reaches the semiparametric efficiency bound: \[\widehat A^*_{\mathrm{AIPW}}(\pi) =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(2\pi(X_i)-1)\Gamma_i^*,\] \[\Gamma_i^*=\mu_1(X_i)-\mu_0(X_i) +W_i\frac{Y_i-\mu_1(X_i)}{e(X_i)} -(1-W_i)\frac{Y_i-\mu_0(X_i)}{1-e(X_i)}.\] Assume further that there are \(o_P(n^{-1/4})\)-consistent regression adjustments \(\widehat\mu_w(x)\) and \(\widehat e(x)\). As \(n\to\infty\), both nuisance-estimation errors are \(o_P(n^{-1/4})\). Construct a doubly robust estimator close to the efficient oracle: \[\widehat A_{\mathrm{AIPW}}(\pi) =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(2\pi(X_i)-1)\widehat\Gamma_i,\] \[\begin{aligned} \widehat\Gamma_i={}&\widehat\mu_1^{(-k(i))}(X_i) -\widehat\mu_0^{(-k(i))}(X_i)\\ &+W_i\frac{Y_i-\widehat\mu_1^{(-k(i))}(X_i)} {\widehat e^{(-k(i))}(X_i)} -(1-W_i)\frac{Y_i-\widehat\mu_0^{(-k(i))}(X_i)} {1-\widehat e^{(-k(i))}(X_i)}. \end{aligned}\] This too is a weighted classification objective. If \(\Pi\) is a VC class, the feasible and oracle objectives are uniformly asymptotically equivalent faster than \(1/\sqrt n\): \[\sqrt n\sup_{\pi\in\Pi} \left|\widehat A_{\mathrm{AIPW}}(\pi) -\widehat A^*_{\mathrm{AIPW}}(\pi)\right| \xrightarrow{P}0.\tag{11.17}\]

Take these rates in \(L_2(P_X)\), with cross-fitting, fixed finite VC dimension, bounded outcomes and estimated propensities bounded away from \(0\) and \(1\). In particular, \(\|\widehat e-e\|_2\max_w\|\widehat\mu_w-\mu_w\|_2=o_P(n^{-1/2})\).

Continuation: regret and efficiency.

Combining the preceding result with empirical-process concentration gives \[R(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{AIPW}}) =O_P\left(\sqrt{\frac{V^*\operatorname{VC}(\Pi)}n}\right), \quad \widehat\pi_{\mathrm{AIPW}} =\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi\in\Pi}\widehat A_{\mathrm{AIPW}}(\pi),\] \[V^*=\mathbb E[\tau^2(X_i)] +\mathbb E\left[\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y_i(0)\mid X_i)}{1-e(X_i)}\right] +\mathbb E\left[\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y_i(1)\mid X_i)}{e(X_i)}\right].\] Athey and Wager (2017 preprint), Policy learning with observational data.

The displayed \(V^*\) decomposition. \(\textcolor{black}{\longrightarrow}\) This bound is optimal when the treatment effect just emerges from the noise.

Not quite right… Here \(V^*=E[(\Gamma_i^*)^2]\) and \(\operatorname{Var}((2\pi(X)-1)\Gamma_i^*)=V^*-A(\pi)^2\). The optimality claim concerns a worst-case model; it is not a pointwise claim for every distribution.

The role of the policy class

Start from a nonparametric model: \(\mu_w(x)\) and \(e(x)\) may be arbitrary. Then the Bayes-optimal treatment assignment rule is \[\pi_{\mathrm{Bayes}}(x)=\mathbf1\{\tau(x)>0\}.\] Our goal, however, is to work with a specified policy class \(\Pi\), not necessarily to approximate this Bayes rule. Examples include linear decision rules, \(k\)-sparse decision rules, and decision trees of depth \(\ell\). We do not require \(\pi_{\mathrm{Bayes}}\in\Pi\).

Reasons:

  1. We use \(X_i\) to make unconfoundedness plausible: \(\{Y_i(0),Y_i(1)\}\perp W_i\mid X_i\). Access to a richer, appropriate set of pretreatment confounders can make this assumption more plausible; adding arbitrary variables, such as colliders, need not do so. For reliable modeling, it is preferable to use richer features and flexible nonparametric models for \(e(x)\) and \(\mu_w(x)\).

  2. A policy has a concrete form and practical restrictions: it may not use certain features; resources or treatment budgets may be limited; and the functional form of \(\pi(\cdot)\) may be constrained.
