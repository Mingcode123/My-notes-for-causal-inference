Chapter 15. Synthetic control

If no single control unit resembles the treated city or state, combine several untreated units into a synthetic comparison. Aggregate cities and states can themselves form a panel. A causal comparison needs untreated donors, no anticipation or spillovers, and a credible stable relation between pre- and post-intervention untreated outcomes.

There are \(J+1\) units. Unit 1 receives treatment; units \(2,\ldots,J+1\) are untreated and form the donor pool. \(Y_{jt}^{I}\) and \(Y_{jt}^{N}\) are the potential outcomes with and without intervention for unit \(j\) at time \(t\). Treatment starts at \(T_0\). For \(t>T_0\), \[\tau_{1t}=Y_{1t}^{I}-Y_{1t}^{N}.\] The treated outcome is factual and the untreated one counterfactual; the effect is a function of time. For weights \(W=(w_2,\ldots,w_{J+1})\), \[\widehat Y_{1t}^{N}=\sum_{j=2}^{J+1}w_jY_{jt}.\] Regression can be used to choose these weights: minimize the squared pre-intervention distance between the treated unit and the weighted donor average.

Synthetic control as regression.

Build a virtual California from donor states. The matrix has years as rows, states as columns, and cigarette sales as entries. Set California’s series to \(Y\), other states’ series to \(X\), and regress \(Y\sim X\). The example weights include \(-0.436\) and \(-0.032\) (39 weights for 39 states). Their weighted sum is the virtual state, chosen to minimize its discrepancy from the treated unit.

This can overfit: the virtual state almost coincides with California before intervention but fluctuates strongly afterward. Ridge or Lasso may help regularize a regression with many donor series.

Avoiding extrapolation.

Earlier overfitting is attributed to extrapolation: weights place observations outside the range of the data. Impose a convex-combination restriction. The synthetic control still has the form \(\widehat Y_{1t}^{N}=\sum_{j=2}^{J+1}w_jY_{jt}\), but choose \(W\) to minimize \[\|X_1-X_0W\|_V= \left\{\sum_{h=1}^{k}v_h\left(X_{h1}-\sum_{j=2}^{J+1}w_jX_{hj}\right)^2\right\}^{1/2}, \qquad w_j\ge0,\quad\sum_{j=2}^{J+1}w_j=1.\] The drawings contrast unconstrained extrapolation from donor points to a treated point outside their triangle with a constrained fit inside the donor convex hull.

\(v_h\) reflects each variable’s importance in matching treatment and synthetic control; different \(v_h\) values yield different weights. Choices mentioned are standardizing each variable to mean zero and unit variance, or giving greater importance to variables that predict \(Y\) better. The implementation here gives every variable equal importance: define a loss function, use SciPy quadratic optimization with weights summing to one, and constrain each weight to \([0,1]\). The resulting example weights are sparse and sum to one (the example vector includes zeros and an approximately \(0.3852\) entry).

Significance and placebo comparisons.

Use a placebo comparison inspired by permutation testing. Refit synthetic control and estimate an effect for each of the 39 states. California gives the actual intervention estimate; the other states give placebo effects. If untreated states also appear to have large effects, that is concerning. Post-intervention variation is larger than pre-intervention variation; some units fit poorly even before intervention and should be excluded. Apply the same pre-period fit threshold to all assignments, using \[\mathrm{MSE}=\frac1N\sum_t(Y_t-\widehat Y_t^{\mathrm{synth}})^2.\] To compare the effects, compute all state effects, then form \[p=\frac1{J_{\rm eligible}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal E}\mathbf1\{\hat\tau_j\le\hat\tau_{\mathrm{California}}\},\] where \(\mathcal E\) contains the eligible assignments, including the observed assignment. For a negative effect, count effects at least as negative. The example suggests that California is extreme among the placebo effects. Calling this an exact \(p\)-value requires an assignment or exchangeability justification; a placebo rank in an observational study is otherwise a diagnostic. Convex weights restrict extrapolation but do not guarantee a good counterfactual fit.
