Chapter 8. Instrumental variables

Suppose \[Y_i=\beta_0+kT_i+\beta W_i+u_i,\] where \(T\) is treatment and \(W\) is a confounder. When \(W_i\) is unavailable, fit \(Y_i=\beta_0+kT_i+v_i\), with \(v_i=\beta W_i+u_i\). Then \(\operatorname{Cov}(T,v)\ne0\), so the estimate of \(k\) is biased. The unobserved term carries information linking \(T\) and \(Y\).

Introduce an instrument \(Z\) related to \(T\) and affecting \(Y\) only through \(T\), with \(\operatorname{Cov}(Z,v)=0\). The graph is \(Z\to T\to Y\), \(W\to T\), \(W\to Y\). Thus \[\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Cov}(Z,Y)&=\operatorname{Cov}(Z,\beta_0+kT+v)\\ &=k\operatorname{Cov}(Z,T)+\operatorname{Cov}(Z,v)=k\operatorname{Cov}(Z,T),\\ k&=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(Z,Y)}{\operatorname{Cov}(Z,T)} =\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(Y,Z)/\operatorname{Var}(Z)}{\operatorname{Cov}(T,Z)/\operatorname{Var}(Z)}. \end{aligned}\] Both numerator and denominator are regression coefficients. The numerator is the reduced-form coefficient: the effect of \(Z\) on \(Y\), operating only through \(T\).

My calculus intuition. \[k=\frac{dY/dZ}{dT/dZ}=\frac{dY}{dZ}\frac{dZ}{dT}=\frac{dY}{dT}.\] For a binary instrument, the Wald estimator is \[k=\frac{E[Y\mid Z=1]-E[Y\mid Z=0]}{E[T\mid Z=1]-E[T\mid Z=0]}.\] I understand this as turning the change from \(Z\to Y\) back into the effect \(T\to Y\).

Using an instrument.

In the example, \(Z_0\) is quarter of birth, \(T\) education, \(W\) ability, and \(Y\) wage. Relevance, \(\operatorname{Cov}(Z,T)\ne0\), can be examined from the first stage. My later correction: “\(Z\) affects \(Y\) only through \(T\)” is an exclusion restriction, not \(Y\perp Z\mid T\). Chapter 9 states it with potential outcomes. Conditioning on \(T\) can open the collider path \(Z\to T\leftarrow W\to Y\).

Regress \(T\sim Z_0\) to check the first-stage relationship, then \(Y\sim Z_0\) to examine the reduced form. Method 1 takes the ratio of these regression coefficients. Method 2 is 2SLS:

  1. Regress \(T\) on \(Z_0,Z_1,Z_2\).

  2. Regress \(Y\) on fitted \(T\) and \(Z_1,Z_2\): \[Y\sim\underbrace{\widehat T}_{\text{fitted from }Z_0,Z_1,Z_2}+Z_1+Z_2.\]

In the formula interface, one can specify \(T\sim Z_0\) because the other terms are automatically added in stage 2. The OLS comparison is written \(Y\sim T+C(Z_0,Z_1,Z_2)\).

A weak first stage makes IV estimation imprecise. With valid, sufficiently strong instruments and a fixed number of instruments, 2SLS is consistent even when OLS is inconsistent. In finite samples it can be biased toward OLS; adding many weak instruments can worsen this problem, so the instrument count alone does not determine a monotone improvement.

instrument, treatment, confounder and outcome.

The derivative cancellation is my intuition for a differentiable single path; the covariance derivation above supplies the argument for this constant-slope model. In the two-stage specification, \(Z_1,Z_2\) are included exogenous controls and belong in both stages.
