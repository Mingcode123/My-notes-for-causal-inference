Chapter 10. Matching

When grouping on confounders to estimate ATE, weight the treatment effect in each group. Weighting by sample size pulls the estimate toward larger groups, even though their treatment variation may be small; this is marked “nonparametric estimation.” Weighting by variance gives higher-variance groups greater weight; this is linked to linear, parametric regression and its emphasis on high-variance covariates.

Subclassification.

If \(X\) creates confounding, group by equal \(X\) values. Under \((Y_0,Y_1)\perp T\mid X\), consistency, and overlap, \[\begin{aligned} \mathrm{ATE}&=\int\{E[Y\mid X=x,T=1]-E[Y\mid X=x,T=0]\}\,dP(x),\\ \widehat{\mathrm{ATE}}&=\sum_{k=1}^{K}(\bar Y_{k1}-\bar Y_{k0})\frac{N_k}{N}. \end{aligned}\] This means averaging over individual covariate values \(\{x_1,\ldots,x_K\}\).

Matching estimator.

Use distances between observations to define similarity, then compare similar or nearby individuals receiving different treatments. K-nearest neighbors. Normalize a coordinate when defining distance: \((x_i-\bar x)/\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(x)}\). \[\widehat{\mathrm{ATE}}=\frac1N\sum_i(2T_i-1)(Y_i-Y_{j(i)}).\] \(j(i)\) is the most similar observation from the other treatment group. The factor \(2T_i-1\) changes the sign according to which treatment group \(i\) belongs to.

Matching bias and correction.

For the treated group, consider \[\widehat{\mathrm{ATET}}=\frac1{N_1}\sum_{i:T_i=1}(Y_i-Y_{j(i)}),\qquad \textcolor{blue}{\mu_0(x)=E[Y\mid X=x,T=0]}.\] The centering discrepancy is written \[B_N=\frac1{N_1}\sum_{i:T_i=1}\{\mu_0(X_i)-\mu_0(X_{j(i)})\},\qquad \sqrt{N_1}B_N,\] and need not converge to zero. Multiplying the discrepancy by \(\sqrt{N_1}\) magnifies it. I compare \(X_i\)’s untreated potential outcome (counterfactual because \(i\) is treated) with \(X_j\)’s observed untreated outcome; \(X_i\approx X_j\) is meant to make their untreated outcomes close. Subtract an estimate of this discrepancy: \[\widehat{\mathrm{ATET}}_{\mathrm{bc}}= \frac1{N_1}\sum_{i:T_i=1}\left[(Y_i-Y_{j(i)})- \{\hat\mu_0(X_i)-\hat\mu_0(X_{j(i)})\}\right].\] \(\hat\mu_0\) is estimated, for example by least squares. A local LS correction does not alter the matching estimator’s original aim.

The curse of dimensionality. More covariates mean larger matching distances: each cell contains fewer observations.

Bias reduction relies on overlap, smoothness, and an adequate regression approximation. Sample-share weighting targets ATE; variance weighting generally targets a different average.
