Chapter 18. Heterogeneous treatment effects and personalization

Unlike ordinary prediction, the aim is to intervene and predict the effect. Let \(X\) contain pre-treatment characteristics (for example, past sales), and let treatment \(T\) be price. The policy target is \(\arg\max_t E[Y(t)\mid X=x]\). Under consistency, conditional exchangeability and overlap, this can be learned from \(E[Y\mid X=x,T=t]\).

Move from ATE to CATE. For binary treatment, ATE is \(E[Y_1-Y_0]\); for continuous treatment, I use \(E[y'(t)]\), the derivative of the outcome with respect to treatment. The conditional versions are \[E[Y_1-Y_0\mid X],\qquad E[y'(t)\mid X].\]

My annotation. Add the condition \(X\): calculate the expectation separately for each \(X\), then choose a personalized policy.

Here “elasticity” refers informally to sensitivity, \(\partial Y_i/\partial T_i\), approximated by \[\frac{Y(T_i)-Y(T_i+\varepsilon)}{T_i-(T_i+\varepsilon)}.\] But both outcomes are unobservable for the same individual: the causal-inference problem. In a simple regression \(y_i=\beta_0+\beta_1t_i+\beta_2X_i+e_i\), the derivative is only \(\beta_1\), a common elasticity. Add an interaction: \[y_i=\beta_0+\beta_1t_i+\beta_2X_i+\beta_3t_iX_i+e_i, \qquad \frac{\partial\hat y_i}{\partial t_i}=\hat\beta_1+\hat\beta_3X_i.\] The interaction makes CATE depend on the covariates. \(\beta_1,\beta_3\) determine the derivative relationship. Another approximation is a finite difference \(\{y(t+\varepsilon)-y(t)\}/\varepsilon\), followed by the practical one-unit approximation \(\hat y(t+1)-\hat y(t)\).

A one-unit difference approximates a derivative only when the response is sufficiently linear over that interval. Dimensionless elasticity is \((t/y)\,\partial y/\partial t=\partial\log y/\partial\log t\).
