Chapter 9. Noncompliance and local average treatment effects

Compliers follow assignment; never-takers never receive treatment; always-takers always receive it; defiers do the opposite. In \(Z\to T\to Y\) with \(W\to T\) and \(W\to Y\), never-takers and always-takers weaken the first stage. For example, never-takers pull the reduced-form outcome difference toward the control condition.

Potential outcomes now have two arguments: \(Y_i(t,z)\); the written example is \(Y_i(1,0)\) for \(T_i=1,Z_i=0\). Potential treatment \(T_{0i}\) corresponds to \(Z_i=0\).

IV assumptions.

  1. \((Y_{i0},Y_{i1},T_{0i},T_{1i})\perp Z_i\). Instrument groups are comparable.

  2. Exclusion: \(Y_i(1,0)=Y_i(1,1)=Y_{i1}\) and \(Y_i(0,0)=Y_i(0,1)=Y_{i0}\). The instrument affects the outcome only via treatment.

  3. \(E[T_{1i}-T_{0i}]\ne0\): the first stage exists; the instrument really affects treatment.

  4. Monotonicity: \(T_{1i}\ge T_{0i}\) almost surely. Some individuals comply, so \(P(T_{1i}>T_{0i})>0\).

Deriving the Wald estimand.

Consider the Wald ratio: \[\frac{E[Y\mid Z=1]-E[Y\mid Z=0]}{E[T\mid Z=1]-E[T\mid Z=0]}.\] Using exclusion and random assignment, \[\begin{aligned} E[Y_i\mid Z_i=1]&=E[Y_{i0}+T_{i1}(Y_{i1}-Y_{i0})\mid Z_i=1]\\ &=E[Y_{i0}+T_{i1}(Y_{i1}-Y_{i0})],\\ E[Y_i\mid Z_i=0]&=E[Y_{i0}+T_{i0}(Y_{i1}-Y_{i0})]. \end{aligned}\] The numerator is \[\begin{aligned} E[(Y_{i1}-Y_{i0})(T_{i1}-T_{i0})] =E[Y_{i1}-Y_{i0}\mid T_{i1}>T_{i0}]P(T_{i1}>T_{i0}), \end{aligned}\] and the denominator is \(E[T_{i1}-T_{i0}]=P(T_{i1}>T_{i0})\). Their ratio is therefore \[E[Y_{i1}-Y_{i0}\mid T_{i1}>T_{i0}].\] The effect is conditional on compliance: this is LATE, not the population ATE. IV identifies the treatment effect only for compliers. Compliers have \(T_{i1}>T_{i0}\); never-takers have \(T_{i1}=T_{i0}=0\); always-takers have \(T_{i1}=T_{i0}=1\). Defiers run counter to assignment.
