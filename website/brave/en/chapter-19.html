Chapter 19. Evaluating causal models

The purpose is prediction and ranking (grouping). One can fit on nonrandom data with valid adjustment, then evaluate on separate randomized data (or a separately identified, adjusted evaluation sample). Compare:

  1. \(\mathrm{sales}_i=\beta_0+\beta_1\mathrm{price}_i+\beta_2X_i+\beta_3X_i\mathrm{price}_i+e_i\), a linear model (the feature terms can be matrices).

  2. \(\mathrm{sales}_i=G(X_i,\mathrm{price}_i)+e_i\), using a regression tree. Check \(R^2\) for overfitting.

  3. A random-number prediction as a placebo comparison.

An elasticity model ranks units by treatment sensitivity. Within a segment, estimate elasticity by a univariate regression \(y_i=\beta_0+\beta_1t_i+e_i\): \[\hat\beta_1=\frac{\sum_i(t_i-\bar t)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sum_i(t_i-\bar t)^2},\] where \(\bar t\) is mean treatment and \(\bar y\) mean outcome.

Cumulative elasticity curve.

Sort by predicted elasticity, from most sensitive downward. Estimate the slope for the first group, then keep adding groups until all observations are included, where it reaches the full-sample slope: \[\widehat{y'}(t)_k=\hat\beta_k= \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(t_i-\bar t_k)(y_i-\bar y_k)}{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(t_i-\bar t_k)^2}.\] My ranking goals are that \(\widehat{y'}(t)_k>\widehat{y'}(t)_{k+a}\) and that the difference be large. The sketch descends toward the overall effect and labels a well-performing ranking as preferable.

Cumulative gain curve.

\[\widehat F(k)=\hat\beta_k\frac{k}{N} =\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(t_i-\bar t_k)(y_i-\bar y_k)}{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(t_i-\bar t_k)^2}\frac{k}{N}.\] The sketch compares a curved gain line with a straight reference. For a confidence interval, the standard error is \[S_{\hat\beta_1}=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_i\hat\varepsilon_i^2}{(n-2)\sum_i(t_i-\bar t)^2}}.\]

cumulative slope (left) and gain against a straight reference (right).

Recompute \(\bar t_k,\bar y_k\) within each prefix. The drawn decreasing curve uses a positive-benefit convention; for negative price effects, rank by more negative slopes or change the sign consistently. The displayed standard error is pointwise under i.i.d. homoskedastic linear errors, not a simultaneous band for the entire curve.
