Chapter 12. Doubly robust estimation

Under consistency, conditional exchangeability and overlap, combine outcome regression and propensity weighting, then average over \(X\): \[\begin{aligned} \widehat{\mathrm{ATE}}={}&\frac1N\sum_i\left[\frac{T_i\{Y_i-\hat\mu_1(X_i)\}}{\hat p(X_i)}+\hat\mu_1(X_i)\right]\\ &-\frac1N\sum_i\left[\frac{(1-T_i)\{Y_i-\hat\mu_0(X_i)\}}{1-\hat p(X_i)}+\hat\mu_0(X_i)\right]. \end{aligned}\] Here \(\hat p(X)\) estimates the propensity score (for example, using logistic regression), and \(\hat\mu_1(X)\) and \(\hat\mu_0(X)\) estimate \(E[Y\mid X,T=1]\) and \(E[Y\mid X,T=0]\). The first average estimates \(E[Y_1]\) and the second \(E[Y_0]\). For consistency, either the propensity model or both treatment-arm outcome regressions must be consistently estimated, with suitable control of estimation error.

My algebraic explanation. For \(E[Y_1]\), \[\widehat E[Y_1]=\frac1N\sum_i\left[\frac{T_i\{Y_i-\hat\mu_1(X_i)\}}{\hat p(X_i)}+\hat\mu_1(X_i)\right].\] If \(\hat p\) is wrong but \(\hat\mu_1\) is correct, the first term is zero for \(T_i=0\) and has mean zero for \(T_i=1\), leaving the regression prediction. The cancellation is conditional: \(E[T\{Y-\mu_1(X)\}/q(X)\mid X]=0\) for a nonzero candidate propensity \(q\).

Alternatively, \[\widehat E[Y_1]=\frac1N\sum_i\left[\frac{T_iY_i}{\hat p(X_i)}-\frac{T_i-\hat p(X_i)}{\hat p(X_i)}\hat\mu_1(X_i)\right].\] If the outcome model is wrong but the propensity model is correct, \(T_i-\hat p(X_i)\) has mean zero conditional on \(X_i\), so the correction vanishes in expectation.

My practical preference is to combine the two models with DRE; the identifying assumptions and the quality of the data still matter.
